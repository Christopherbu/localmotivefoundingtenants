What Localmotive is

Localmotive is built on one belief: that human connection, done honestly, is the most powerful force there is. A real conversation between two people being straight with each other still moves things that nothing else can. Communication, relationships, influence earned rather than taken, and being authentically ourselves with whoever is in front of us. That is what we build on, and that is what carries the value.

We do it with energy, honesty and genuine curiosity, because that is what people respond to and it is the only version of this worth doing. The people this attracts are switched on, self aware and good with others, and the business is built to reward exactly that.

The name says it. Local, and motive. It is an engine, and everyone it touches, the workshops, the customers and us, is meant to move forward because of it.

The leanest, most sensible, highest standard version of this business

Built to support everyone in it. Every rule is designed so the business is done the way that actually works: sold straight, run properly, paid on time, and rewarding the person who did the work. The point of all of it is to get each of us to as much financial freedom as this can give, as fast as it can honestly be done.

03The Five Rules

1
One

Your doors are your doors

$167 on every card you personally sell, $170 once the business has paid for its year at card 501, counted across everyone. Neither of us earns a cent off the other's own sales.

2
Two

Whoever trains the rep keeps the override

The person who recruits, trains and manages a rep is the person who earns from that rep. Your reps are yours, mine are mine. Build half the team, you own half the overrides.

The same rule applies one level down. When one of your reps reaches rung four and starts building their own team, they take the leader override on their people and you keep what is left underneath them. You are paid for building the person who built the team. Nobody above takes a share of what someone below has earned.

3
Three

$15 a rep card to whoever manages the workshop

Every campaign has one named manager, written on the campaign sheet the day it starts. Default is whoever signed it. The manager does the restocking, the update emails, the workshop calls and anything that comes up at that shop.

The manager takes $15 on every rep card sold at that workshop, either of our reps, uncapped, for the life of the campaign. It comes out of the trainer's share, because a trainer's override only exists while someone keeps that workshop alive and stocked.

Never on our own doors. Whatever you sell yourself is the full $167, at any workshop, whoever signed it. Neither of us takes a cent off the other's knocking, ever.

Changing manager is a request, not an argument. If you are the one doing the work, say so and take the name. The other person either picks the job back up or agrees, and the money moves with the name. Nobody has to prove anything about anybody.

Paid weekly, on the invoice, like everything else. Count the rep cards sold at workshops you manage that week, multiply by $15, add it as a line. Nothing accrues, nothing waits for the end of a campaign, and there is no cap. If reps are still selling there, someone is still restocking and taking the calls, so the fee keeps running for as long as the campaign does.

Why we pay for managing rather than for signing. The reward for signing a workshop is the ground itself. A campaign is 200 cards and neither of us can knock that alone, so every workshop signed is doors for both of us at full commission. Without new workshops we both run out of ground and earn nothing, which makes signing the thing that keeps us both working rather than a favour to anybody. Managing is different. It is small, ongoing, easy to let slide, and it is the job that quietly lands on whoever cares most. So that is the one with money attached, and the money scales with rep activity because that is exactly when the managing actually takes effort.
4
Four

Nobody owns a campaign

  • Each workshop's area is mapped when it is signed. That map is the campaign.
  • Each of us places our own team, ourselves included. Before the week starts we agree who is on which streets.
  • Cannot agree? Whoever sold the most last week picks first, counting their whole team. Team sales, not personal, so nobody is punished for spending a week recruiting instead of knocking. Only tiebreak, never needs discussing.
  • Nobody blocks the other from a workshop. A campaign is 200 cards deep and neither of us can knock that alone.
  • A street is worked and then it is done. Nobody holds a street they are not knocking.
5
Five

Refunds sit with the seller

A card cancelled in the cooling off period is refunded in full, and every piece of that card reverses. Nobody funds anyone else's cancellations, and the business cannot carry them either. On your own door, the $167 comes off your next invoice. On a rep sale, every layer reverses: the rep's commission off the rep's next pay, the $15 off the campaign manager, the leader override off the leader, and what is left off the trainer. If a rep has already gone, the trainer wears the rep's piece. Keep a few hundred back in your first month so a bad week never leaves you short. A card sold straight does not cancel, which is why this rule and the selling standard are the same thing seen from two sides.

04One Name On The Door

Everything above is dead even. This is the one place it is not, so it gets a straight explanation rather than a quiet one.

  • The call has to come from one place. Most weeks there is an obvious answer and we both see it. Some weeks there is not, and something like this needs one person who makes the call and who the buck stops with. Two owners with equal say and no tiebreak do not compromise, they stall, and stalling is how good mates end up not speaking. One clear answer on who decides protects the friendship more than it constrains it.
  • A standard has to come from one place too, or it is not a standard. How we sell, how we log, how people are paid. If that comes from two places it drifts, and businesses like this fall apart fast when it drifts. So it is set once, written down, and held by everyone including Chris.
  • It is an agreed standard, and it stays open. Both of us signed off on it. When either of us sees a better way, it gets raised, and if it is better it gets adopted. Coming from one place does not mean coming from one person's mood. It means one version of it exists.
  • Every call gets made for the business, not for Chris. The test for any decision is whether it keeps the business alive and paying both of us. That is the only job the director role has, and it is the reason the role exists.
  • Someone's name has to be on the risk. The company, the consumer law obligations, the workshop agreements, the merchant account. If a workshop closes mid campaign, every cardholder has a claim. That cannot land on nobody, and it cannot land on two people either.
  • It started as Chris's. It was his initiative. He decided to go, put the pieces together and started knocking. Joel has been part of it from the start and has shaped it, so it is not a solo project. But it began as Chris's push, and if he is going to build it at all, there is a way he wants to build it. That is not a judgement on anyone else's way. It is just his.
  • It is not control over Joel. Nothing in the five rules gives Chris a say over Joel's hours, reps, rates or workshops. If it ever feels that way, it is a mistake, and it should be said out loud.
  • Nothing is paid for holding it. Chris earns the same $167 and the same overrides as Joel, and nothing for the admin, the liability or the ownership. He also fronts the setup cash, about $1,850, and the reserve pays it back before anything else.
  • Joel loses nothing by this. He works it as a partner in every practical way, every rule pays him identically, and we are still in this together. The question gets looked at again in twelve months, when we both know what this is.
Why we are doing this together at all. Because it is much easier, and we go a lot further, backing each other than either of us would alone. This is get in, get out, for both of us. One name on the door is the version where nothing gets in either of our way while we do that. That is the whole reason, and it is the same reason for both of us.
What all of these rules are actually for. They are not a rulebook. They are a system we follow so that nothing has to be argued twice, nothing depends on remembering, and nothing quietly builds up between us. Every rule in here exists to make the work simpler, the money cleaner, and the friendship safer. That is the whole point, and if any rule ever stops doing that, it is the rule that changes, not the friendship.

05What This Asks, And Where The Money Is

What this asks of us

  • Full attention, for twelve months. Being in this at this level, on full commission with every override and every royalty, is a founding position, and a founding position deserves as much of us as we can give it. Not every waking hour. As much as we honestly can.
  • The goal is simple and shared. Make as much money as we can over the next twelve months, have a good life while we do it, and come out the other side with something actually changed. Both of us.
  • Show up on the days it is hard. Every door to door business is decided in the third week of a bad month, when it would be easier not to go out. That is the week that counts.
  • Keep the standard when nobody is watching. The log, the receipt, the cooling off paperwork, the straight sale. Done every time or it is not a standard.
  • Talk every week. Ten minutes on Friday when the pays go out. What sold, what did not, what is next, anything that needs saying. Small and regular beats big and rare.

Where the money actually is

  • Knocking the door. Nothing in this document earns a dollar until someone is standing on a doorstep. Everything else exists to put a person there and pay them properly for it. When in doubt, go knock.
  • Workshops times reps. That is the whole growth equation. Every workshop is 200 cards of ground. Every rep is another set of feet on it. Grow both, in that order.
  • Signing workshops is the constraint. Reps are easier to find than good workshops to put them on. The person out signing mechanics is unlocking everyone's income, which is exactly why rule three pays them for it.
  • A clean sale is worth more than a fast one. A card that cancels costs the seller $167 and the business its reputation on that street. The standard is not a brake on the money, it is the money.
  • Overrides are the exit. Own doors pay the week. Reps pay the year. The person with three trained reps is earning while they sleep, and that is the version of this that actually changes a life.

What an hour is worth

Every hour in this business goes to one of these. Gold compounds. Grey pays once or protects. Neither is optional, but the order matters when time is short.

And a back half of the year where a team of three, none of them stars, is putting nine thousand a week in front of you. Cut every number in this by a third and it still clears a $60k debt and a thousand a week to live on inside the year. That is the vision, and it is made of forty ordinary weeks.

01How We Run It

Getting paid

  • Chris does the pays. One ledger, every card matched to a customer, a workshop, a seller and an invoice. That is how the reserve, the GST, the refunds and the royalties all get checked, and the ledger is open to both of us at any time.
  • If Joel would rather pay himself and his reps straight from the Zeller account, that is fine. The rule is the same for both of us: once a week, exactly the amount on that week's invoice, never more, never early. Done properly, it is the same thing.
  • Paid the Friday after the week the cards were sold. Most companies hold reps' pay for two or three weeks against cancellations. We hold one. By the time money moves, 7 to 12 days of the cooling off period have already run, so nearly every cancellation shows up before anyone is paid. That protects both of us and it is why rule five almost never has to be used.
  • Invoicing takes thirty seconds. Tell Claude how many cards you sold, it builds the invoice from the template and sends it. Sooner than Friday in a given week if you need it, just ask.
  • Every sale logged the day it happens, by end of the week at the latest. Customer, workshop, card number, who sold it. This is part of the job, not admin on top of it. The workshop is owed a record of who holds their cards and so is the law.
  • Card stubs into the live sheet weekly, the one shared with each workshop.

Where the money lives

A reserve that is just a number inside the account we pay people from is not a reserve. So every card is split into separate accounts every Friday, and Zeller empties to zero. If it is at zero on Friday night, the split happened. If it is not, something is wrong and it is visible straight away.

  • The reserve account has no card attached. Nothing goes in except the Friday transfer. Nothing comes out except a business cost, and every withdrawal gets a line in the reserve ledger saying what it paid for.
  • The reserve ledger is one sheet with three numbers. Collected this year. Spent this year, each item named. Balance. It lives in the shared Drive.
  • The trigger for $170 is a number on that sheet. When collected this year reaches $4,330, which is 500 cards across the whole business, the reserve drops to $5.65 and commission goes to $170. No judgement call, no conversation, it is just the ledger.
  • The year resets each September. Reserve goes back to $8.65 until that year's costs are covered again, then drops. Every year after the first is cheaper, so the raise comes earlier each year.
  • Joel sees all of it. The Friday ledger summary carries the reserve balance and the collected-this-year figure every week. The reserve account statement goes in the shared Drive monthly. Any withdrawal can be checked against the ledger at any time.

Cash

  • Logged in Zeller as a cash sale on the spot, so the receipt goes out the same way and the cooling off clock starts.
  • Banked within 48 hours. A cash sale that cancels is refunded by bank transfer, same day, and logged against the original sale.
  • Never used to pay yourself directly. Through the invoice like everything else.

Selling standard

  • Sell it completely straight. Labour discounts only. They pay for parts. They pay for any labour beyond what is on the card. No surprises at the counter.
  • Own your hours and how you carry yourself. Legal window is 9 to 6 Monday to Friday, 9 to 5 Saturday, no Sundays or public holidays. Inside that, use your judgement and respect the street.
  • Ten business day cooling off, in writing, every time. The voucher is the agreement, so it gets filled in properly and the customer signs the back before you leave the door. No signature, no sale.
  • We are Localmotive at the door, separate from the workshop.
  • Nothing promised that is not written down.

02What Comes Next

Who owns what from here, and the first moves in order.

The roles

Two roles. Both of us hold the first one identically. Only one of us holds the second, and section ten says why.

Partner
Chris and Joel, the same
  • Sells. Own doors, at any workshop, full commission.
  • Signs workshops. Finds them, calls them, meets them, sets the values, gets the agreement signed.
  • Manages the campaigns they sign. Orders the print run, sets the start date, keeps the customer sheet, restocks, takes the workshop's calls.
  • Builds a team. Recruits, trains, places and manages their own reps.
  • Holds the standard. Sells straight, logs same day, keeps the door hours, on every door.
Director
Chris only
  • Holds the entity. The company, the ABN, the insurance, the accountant, BAS.
  • Runs the ledger and the pays. Every card matched, every Friday paid, the reserve kept.
  • Holds the accounts. Zeller, the bank, the money in and out.
  • Sets the standard. Decides what the selling standard and the door rules are. Everyone including him then holds them.
  • Makes the call when there is no obvious answer, and writes it down for September.

Nobody owns a workshop, a campaign, a lead or a customer. The business does. The brand, the card design, the print tools, the customer sheets, the lead list, the templates and the app are all business property, used by both of us and left behind by either of us. A partner manages the campaign they signed. They do not own it.

  • Chris knocks Brendale for AVR. The live campaign keeps moving while everything else gets set up.
  • Joel signs a workshop close to home and starts knocking it. His first campaign, his first royalty stream, close enough to work every day.
  • We build the lead list together. Workshops mapped by area, starting local and working outward. Every one we sign is ground for both teams.

  • The company gets set up out of the reserve once the first cards have paid for it, and before the first rep is placed. The business pays for its own entity.
  • Reps come after workshops. A rep with nowhere to knock is a cost. A workshop with nobody knocking is a missed campaign. Get the ground first.

03The Systems That Run It

Each of these exists already or is being built now. Nothing here is a plan to build a system one day. Together they are the business running itself, so the only thing left for either of us to do is the work that pays.

Leads

Workshop leads sheet

One place for every workshop we are chasing. Phone, a one tap Call link, a Map link that opens the shop on Google Maps, contact, owner, status, next action and due date. Status moves it through the pipeline and the sheet tells you when a lead has gone quiet or a follow up is overdue. Works on a phone in the car or on a laptop with the one pager open beside it.

Pipeline

Pipeline view

Counts by status and by who owns it, live workshops, signed but not yet live, conversion from first call to signature, and how many follow ups are overdue. Reads straight off the leads sheet, nothing to type. This is the Friday ten minutes on one screen.

Process

Sign up process

Seven steps from finding a workshop to its first day on the doors: find, call, meet, values, agree, print, launch. Each with what happens, what it produces and what status it moves to, so we gather the same information every time and nothing gets missed. It is also a sale. Turning up with a process, the same one every time, is what makes an owner believe we have done this before and can be trusted with their customers. Same steps, same order, whoever is doing it.

Cards

Card pipeline

Edit the workshop's values in one file, render to print ready PDF, fonts flattened, batch merged for the printer. A new workshop's cards go from values meeting to Kwik Kopy in an afternoon. Print sized 150 to 300 per campaign.

Sales

Customer log and customer sheets

Every card logged the day it sells: customer, workshop, card number, who sold it. Each workshop has its own live sheet showing exactly who holds a card, updated weekly, so they can honour it at the counter and we can prove it if anyone asks.

Pay

Invoices and the ledger

Tell Claude how many cards you sold and it writes the invoice from the template and sends it. One ledger matches every card to a sale, a seller, a royalty and an invoice, so the reserve, the GST and the refunds all reconcile without anyone doing sums.

Money

Friday split

Every Friday the week's Zeller total splits the same way: GST set aside, pays out, reserve stays, the rest to where it belongs. The business never holds money it does not own and never spends money it does not have.

Reps

Sales academy

The training app every rep goes through before a door: the door flow, objections, a roleplay simulator, the economics calculator, the coaching frameworks. Every rep, either of ours, starts from the same standard.

Compliance

Compliance pack

The customer agreement with the ten business day cooling off, the workshop agreement, the selling standard, the door hours. Written once, used on every sale, kept current in one place.

Simple systems, set up before the first new campaign

None of these take more than an afternoon. All of them cause real problems if they do not exist on day one.

  • One inbox. Every workshop and customer email goes through thelocalmotive.au@gmail.com, shared between us. Nothing lives in a personal inbox. If one of us is away, the other can see the whole conversation.
  • A work number each. Chris has a business eSIM already. Joel gets his own, running as a second line on his existing phone, so there is no second handset to carry. Amaysim does prepaid eSIM for a few dollars a month and activates the same day. Personal numbers never go on a card, an agreement or a one pager. Whoever runs a campaign puts their work number on it and answers it for that campaign, so a customer or a workshop always reaches the person who knows their situation.
  • A customer sheet per workshop. Card number, customer name, date sold, expiry, status. Shared with the workshop from the Localmotive Google account so the counter can honour a card in ten seconds. Built from one template, so every workshop's sheet looks the same. Updated weekly by whoever runs the campaign.
  • The cancellation steps. Customer calls inside the cooling off period: log it the same day, refund through Zeller, mark the card cancelled on the workshop sheet, note it against the seller for the next invoice. Four steps, written once, so it is never a judgement call at the time.
  • Email templates. First contact, agreement attached, cards ready and start date, restock, cancellation acknowledged. Five short templates so every workshop hears from us the same way.
  • One Google account for all of it. Everything lives under thelocalmotive.au@gmail.com: the inbox, the Drive, every customer sheet, the lead list, the templates. A folder per workshop with its agreement, values sheet, card artwork and customer sheet. Finance in its own folder. Both of us have the login, both of us can find anything without asking, and nothing sits in a personal account.
  • The Friday checklist. Reconcile Zeller against the log. Invoices in. Pays out. Split done. Ten minutes on the numbers. Same list every week so nothing is forgotten on a busy one.
  • A named manager per campaign. Written on the campaign sheet the day it starts, so there is never a question about who is restocking, emailing the workshop and taking their calls. Changing it is a request, not a debate.
  • The turf map per campaign. The streets that belong to each workshop, drawn once when it is signed, so placement each week is pointing at a map rather than describing an area.
  • Rep onboarding checklist. ABN, agreement signed, academy complete, one ride along, one supervised day, then solo. Same list for either of our reps.
  • Identification at the door. A Localmotive card or lanyard for everyone knocking, so we are visibly Localmotive and visibly separate from the workshop, which the selling standard requires.
